Gout FAQ 2026: the four questions patients actually ask

Answered with 2024-2026 sources. Last updated November 2026. Not medical advice — see your doctor for treatment.

1. What foods trigger gout flares?

30-second answer: Beer, organ meats, certain seafood (sardines, anchovies, shellfish), and fructose-sugary drinks are the strongest documented triggers — but triggers are individual, and your own pattern is what matters.

The generic "avoid list" is where every gout patient starts, and where confusion begins: one 2023-2026 review of patient communities found users hand-building spreadsheets to correlate their own meals with flares 3-5 days later, because generic lists didn't match their experience (r/gout, u/waffadoodle's month-table post, Oct 2023).

Modern guidance agrees plant purines (beans, tofu, mushrooms) are not the concern older books claim — the shift away from blanket vegetable restrictions is explicitly noted in current patient materials (OTCFood 2026 review). What survives scrutiny: beer, spirits, organ meats, small oily fish, shellfish, and fructose drinks.

Sources: Arthritis Australia "gout and diet" (2026); OTCFood myths review (2026); ACR 2020 Guideline food tables; r/gout community analysis (GummySearch, Oct 2026).

2. What should my uric acid target be?

30-second answer: Below 6.0 mg/dL (360 µmol/L). Below 5.0 mg/dL (300 µmol/L) if you have tophi or frequent attacks.

Only 40% of diagnosed patients know their urate level (Gout Education Society national survey, May 2026, n=1,250) — knowing your number and your target is step one of treat-to-target management. Watch units: US labs use mg/dL, most of Europe µmol/L, New Zealand mmol/L (0.36 = 6.0 mg/dL). A tracker that auto-converts prevents the most common "is my number good?" confusion.

Sources: ACR 2020 Guideline; GES/Wakefield survey May 2026; Healthify NZ unit guidance (2026).

3. Does beer really cause flares more than other alcohol?

30-second answer: Beer yes — strongest documented association. But all alcohol raises urate, so total reduction beats only-quitting-beer.

Beer's extra effect likely comes from guanosine, a plant purine specific to beer, stacked on alcohol's own urate-raising mechanism. Spirit drinkers are not exempt: ethanol itself slows uric acid excretion regardless of the drink. The practical target is reducing total alcohol, gradually — which is also more sustainable than cliff-edge quitting (behavior-design evidence, Fogg 2019).

Sources: Arthritis Australia (2026); alcohol-urate mechanism reviews (2024-2026 consensus); r/gout trigger discussions (2026).

4. How do I prepare for a 15-minute gout doctor visit?

30-second answer: Bring four things: your urate trend (not single numbers), a dated flare history, your medication list with adherence estimate, and three written questions.

The problem is real: a 2024 qualitative study across New Zealand and Spain found patients described leaving visits with questions unasked and gout "as the boss" of their working life (RMD Open, 2024). Multiple gout apps now offer a one-page doctor report — evaluation studies show structured self-management summaries improve what gets covered in short visits (JMIR mHealth testing, 2018; still the framework used in 2026 app comparisons).

Sources: Diaz-Torne et al., RMD Open 2024; JMIR mHealth gout app testing 2018; doctor-visit-prep guides (2026).
